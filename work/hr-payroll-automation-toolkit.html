The problem

Every month, attendance and overtime arrived from several construction sites, and no two sites used the same layout. Someone had to copy it all into the payroll and cost sheets, reformat it for the ERP, and then turn it into a WPS salary file.

Every one of those steps was manual. It was slow, it was repetitive, and the risk was real: one wrong MOL number or bank account and a salary transfer gets rejected, which means an employee waiting for money they've earned.

What I built

I wrote a personal macro library that lives inside Excel, so it's available in every workbook I open. I took each step of the payroll cycle and turned it into a one-click tool. Each one asks for confirmation before it runs, so nothing changes by accident.

This is how a payroll month runs now:

  1. Collect site dataAttendance, overtime and leave from each site, formatted the same way.
  2. Build payrollData matched by employee ID into payroll, bonus and cost sheets per site.
  3. Check the salary fileEvery MOL number, routing code and account checked before WPS.
  4. Upload and reportERP upload files, approval tracking and PDF reports in one click.

Results

  • Most of the repetitive payroll and ERP preparation now runs on macros instead of by hand.
  • Bank and MOL errors are caught before the salary transfer, not after it bounces.
  • Data follows one format across all sites, and between payroll and the ERP.

What I learned

Automation works best when it follows how HR actually works. I didn't start with a big plan. Every tool began as one annoying step in a real payroll month, which is why the whole thing fits the way the work is really done.
