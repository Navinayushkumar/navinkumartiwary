How UAE gratuity is calculated: a practical guide with examples

The formula, what counts as wage, the cap, and the checks I run before every final settlement.

By Navin Kumar Tiwary24 Oct 20265 min read

End-of-service gratuity is one of the most common questions HR receives, and one of the easiest places to make a costly mistake. Here is how the calculation works for private-sector employees under the current UAE Labour Law, with two worked examples.

Who is entitled

Under Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, a private-sector employee who completes at least one year of continuous service is entitled to gratuity at the end of employment. UAE and GCC nationals are normally covered by their pension schemes instead.

What salary is used

Gratuity is based on the last basic salary only. Housing, transport and other allowances, overtime, bonuses and commissions are not included. This is why the basic salary written in the contract matters so much.

The formula

First find the daily basic wage, then apply the number of days for each year of service.

Daily basic wage = Monthly basic salary ÷ 30
  • First 5 years: 21 days of basic wage for each year
  • Each year after 5: 30 days of basic wage for each year
  • Part of a year: paid in proportion, once the first full year is completed
  • Maximum: total gratuity cannot exceed 24 months of basic salary

Example 1: seven years of service

Basic salary AED 4,000, service exactly 7 years. Daily basic wage = 4,000 ÷ 30 = AED 133.33.

Example 2: three and a half years

Basic salary AED 3,000, service 3 years and 6 months. Daily basic wage = AED 100.

3.5 years × 21 days × AED 100 = AED 7,350

Common mistakes

  • Using gross salary instead of basic salary.
  • Counting days of unpaid leave as service. Unpaid absence is not counted.
  • Reducing gratuity because the employee resigned. The current law no longer applies the old resignation reductions.
  • Forgetting to prorate the final partial year.

My checklist before every final settlement

  1. Confirm the joining date against the MOHRE contract, not only the internal record.
  2. Confirm the last basic salary in payroll matches the latest contract or amendment.
  3. Deduct any days of unpaid leave from total service.
  4. Split service into the first 5 years and the years after, then prorate the last partial year.
  5. Check the total against the 24-month cap.
  6. Show any amounts the employee owes as separate, documented deductions.
  7. Pay all end-of-service dues within 14 days of the employment ending.

Getting gratuity right protects both the employee and the company. A clear calculation sheet, shared with the employee, also prevents most disputes before they start.

This article is general information based on Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 for the private sector. Free zones such as DIFC and ADGM follow their own rules. For a specific case, check with MOHRE or a legal advisor.
